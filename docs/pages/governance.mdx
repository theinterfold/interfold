# Interfold Governance

This page is the reference for how voting power, proposals, voting, and execution currently work in
the Interfold DAO. It is for FOLD holders, delegates, and ciphernode operators who take part in
governance. At the end, you know where voting power comes from, how a proposal moves to execution,
and which parameters apply.

Interfold governance is the decision-making system for the Interfold DAO. The DAO is built on
[Aragon OSx](https://aragon.org/osx). Governance uses FOLD voting power, and it is centered on
receipt-free secret-ballot voting through [CRISP](/CRISP/introduction).

## Governance at a glance

The standard Interfold governance proposal is an IPP (Interfold Protocol Proposal). An IPP uses
CRISP for receipt-free secret-ballot voting.

A FIPP (Fallback Interfold Protocol Proposal) gives a transparent voting path if CRISP cannot be
used. FIPPs are strictly a fallback mechanism, not an alternative default voting mode.

The current proposal lifecycle is:

**Proposal → Community vote → Foundation approval → Execution**

<Lifecycle label='Proposal lifecycle'>
  <Stage title='Proposal' actor='Proposer'>

A participant with at least 12K FOLD of active voting power creates a proposal. Voting power for the
proposal comes from the snapshot immediately before proposal creation.

  </Stage>
  <Stage title='Community vote' actor='FOLD voters'>

Eligible FOLD voting power decides whether the proposal passes. An IPP uses a secret ballot through
CRISP. A FIPP uses transparent on-chain voting.

  </Stage>
  <Stage title='Foundation approval' actor='Foundation Board'>

A passed proposal does not execute automatically. The Interfold Foundation Board reviews it before
implementation. When the Board approves implementation, the proposal becomes a DAO Resolution.

  </Stage>
  <Stage title='Execution'>

The DAO Resolution is implemented or executed in accordance with the governing documents of the
Foundation and the configured Aragon OSx rules.

  </Stage>
</Lifecycle>

The sections below describe each part of this process.

## Voting power

Voting power comes from committed FOLD, not simply FOLD held in a wallet. Each committed FOLD counts
1:1 toward voting power.

FOLD can contribute voting power in three ways:

| Source       | What it is                                   | When it counts               |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Locked FOLD  | FOLD locked through the governance interface | When it is delegated         |
| Bonded FOLD  | FOLD bonded to a ciphernode                  | Automatically, no delegation |
| Vesting FOLD | Eligible vesting FOLD                        | Automatically, no delegation |

A transferable FOLD balance held in a wallet does not provide voting power unless it is committed
through one of these mechanisms.

Locked, bonded, and vesting FOLD have the same governance weight once active: **1 FOLD = 1 vote.**

### Locked FOLD

FOLD can be locked through the
[Interfold governance interface](https://governance.theinterfold.com/) to create a voting position.
Locked FOLD contributes voting power only when it has been delegated. The lock remains under the
control of the wallet that created it.

**Delegation.** Locked FOLD can be delegated to the holder's own address or to another address. The
delegate can be changed later. Delegation applies to the current and future locks of the holder.
Delegation transfers voting power, not ownership of the underlying FOLD.

**Unlocking.** Locked FOLD can be unlocked at any time, subject to a 30-day delay:

1. Start the withdrawal. Voting power associated with the withdrawing FOLD stops immediately.
2. Wait through the 30-day cooldown.
3. Claim the FOLD when the cooldown ends.

### Bonded FOLD

FOLD bonded to a ciphernode counts automatically toward governance voting power. It does not require
separate delegation.

Bonded FOLD is already committed to network participation. Ciphernode operators post economic
collateral to participate in the network. They are subject to a 30-day exit delay when they
decommission their ciphernodes. The ciphernode system also uses sUSDS-denominated tickets, which
contribute to committee sortition weight.

See the [ciphernode documentation](/operate) for bonding, tickets, committee selection, and operator
penalties.

### Vesting FOLD

Eligible vesting FOLD counts automatically toward governance voting power. It does not require
delegation.

### How the contracts add the three sources

The `BondedVotes` contract computes the voting power of an account. It reads all three sources at
the same timepoint and adds them:

- **Locked FOLD:** the voting power that the voting escrow gives the account. This part follows
  delegation.
- **Bonded FOLD:** the bonded total of the account as a bond owner, from the `BondedCheckpoints`
  contract.
- **Vesting FOLD:** FOLD that is still under a lock policy in the FOLD contract. The contract
  subtracts the part that the bond already covers, and caps the result at the wallet balance.

<Callout type='info'>
  Bonded voting power belongs to the bond owner of a ciphernode, not to the operator key. The
  `BondedVotes` contract does not let an account delegate bonded voting power.
</Callout>

## Voting weight and eligibility

The voting weight of a participant is determined by active FOLD voting power. **1 FOLD of active
voting power = 1 vote.** Locked, bonded, and vesting FOLD are not weighted differently once active.

### Eligibility to vote

Any non-Foundation holder with active committed FOLD voting power is eligible to vote.

### Voting-power snapshot

Voting power for a proposal is determined from the snapshot taken immediately before proposal
creation (proposal creation timestamp − 1). The governance interface displays:

- The voting power of the connected wallet at the snapshot.
- The total voting power eligible for the proposal.

Changes to voting power after the snapshot do not change voting weight for that proposal.

The FOLD token uses timestamps as its clock (`CLOCK_MODE` is `mode=timestamp`). The CRISP program
records the snapshot of a round as `clock() - 1` in the transaction that requests the E3. The
requester cannot choose a different timepoint.

## Proposal types

Interfold governance uses CRISP-based secret-ballot voting as its standard governance path.
Transparent voting exists as a fallback mechanism, not as a co-equal voting mode.

The underlying FOLD voting-power rules are the same for both proposal types.

|                        | IPP                                                 | FIPP                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full name              | Interfold Protocol Proposal                         | Fallback Interfold Protocol Proposal                                            |
| Role                   | The standard proposal type                          | The transparent fallback proposal type                                          |
| When it is used        | By default                                          | Strictly when CRISP is unavailable, or an issue prevents the IPP voting process |
| Voting                 | Receipt-free secret ballot through CRISP            | Transparent on-chain voting                                                     |
| Individual votes       | Never revealed                                      | Visible on-chain                                                                |
| Tally                  | Computed under encryption, then threshold-decrypted | Visible on-chain                                                                |
| Voting-power model     | Committed FOLD, 1 FOLD = 1 vote                     | The same as IPP                                                                 |
| Lifecycle after voting | Foundation approval, then execution                 | The same as IPP                                                                 |

### IPP: Interfold Protocol Proposal

An IPP is the standard Interfold governance proposal.

IPPs use [CRISP (Coercion-Resistant Impartial Selection Protocol)](/CRISP/introduction), the
reference implementation of the Interfold for receipt-free secret-ballot voting. CRISP applies the
confidential coordination model of the Interfold to governance. Private ballots contribute to a
shared result, and individual votes are not exposed.

Ballots are encrypted client-side and tallied under encryption within an Encrypted Execution
Environment (E3). Individual votes are never revealed.

A CRISP voting round proceeds broadly as follows:

1. A CRISP voting round is initiated.
2. A ciphernode committee is formed.
3. The committee performs distributed key generation (DKG) to establish a shared encryption key.
4. Eligible participants submit encrypted ballots.
5. The tally is computed over encrypted votes.
6. The final tally is threshold-decrypted.
7. The outcome can be verified without exposing individual ballots.

No single ciphernode controls output decryption.

CRISP therefore separates ballot visibility from outcome verifiability. Individual choices remain
confidential, and the resulting decision remains verifiable.

#### Changing a vote

A participant can submit a new encrypted vote before voting closes. Only the valid vote in effect
when voting closes is included in the final tally.

#### Vote masking and receipt-freeness

IPPs also support vote masking. Vote masking is a mechanism that makes observable ballot activity
unreliable as evidence of how someone voted.

A mask creates a valid encrypted update without changing the underlying vote. Genuine vote updates
and masks are computationally indistinguishable. For this reason, an outside observer cannot
reliably determine whether visible activity reflects the true or final choice of a voter.

This helps CRISP provide receipt-freeness. Voters cannot produce reliable proof of how they voted,
even if their on-chain activity is observed.

This
[technical blog post](https://blog.theinterfold.com/vote-masking-receipt-freeness-secret-ballots/)
covers the detailed masking mechanics.

### FIPP: Fallback Interfold Protocol Proposal

A FIPP is the transparent fallback proposal type.

FIPPs are used strictly when CRISP is unavailable or an issue prevents the standard IPP voting
process from being used. Individual votes and tallies are visible on-chain.

FIPPs use the same FOLD voting-power model as IPPs. They proceed through the same governance
lifecycle after voting.

## Proposal lifecycle

Interfold governance currently uses a multi-stage process:

**Proposal → Community vote → Foundation approval → Execution**

### Creating proposals

Governance participants with at least 12K FOLD of active voting power can create proposals through
the [Interfold governance interface](https://governance.theinterfold.com/).

The standard proposal type is an IPP, which uses CRISP for receipt-free secret-ballot voting. FIPPs
exist only as a fallback when CRISP cannot be used.

### Community vote

After a proposal is created, eligible FOLD voting power determines whether it passes its voting
stage.

IPPs use receipt-free secret-ballot voting through CRISP. FIPPs use transparent voting only when the
fallback mechanism is required.

### Foundation approval

A proposal that passes the community vote does not execute automatically. It moves to Foundation
Approval, where the Interfold Foundation Board reviews the proposal before implementation.

Under the governing framework of the Foundation, the Board is expected to approve implementation.
The exception is when the Board reasonably determines that a defined constraint applies. For
example, a defined constraint applies when implementation would:

- Be unlawful, unsafe, or impracticable.
- Be inconsistent with the purposes or governing documents of the Foundation.
- Breach a binding obligation.
- Create material legal, regulatory, security, solvency, or operational risk.

If one of those grounds applies, the Board may reject, defer, condition, or return the proposal for
modification. The Board must publish its reasons.

When the Board approves implementation, the passed proposal becomes a DAO Resolution.

### Execution

A DAO Resolution can be implemented or executed in accordance with the governing documents of the
Foundation and the configured Aragon OSx rules.

## DAO and Foundation authority

The Interfold DAO and the Foundation have distinct roles within the governance system.

Token holders can submit and vote on proposals about matters within the purposes of the Foundation.
Passed proposals that complete Foundation review become DAO Resolutions. The Directors are
responsible for implementing DAO Resolutions, subject to their legal and fiduciary duties.

The DAO also has direct governance powers over parts of the structure of the Foundation:

- Electing Directors.
- Changing the size of the Board.
- Removing Directors. Director-removal votes do not require Board approval.

Certain matters may also be designated as Reserved Matters. The Foundation cannot act on a Reserved
Matter without prior approval through a DAO Resolution.

### Interfold Constitution

The Interfold Constitution is the charter of the Interfold DAO. It defines the governing principles
and processes of the DAO, and the relationship of the DAO with the Interfold Foundation.

Protocol users do not adopt the Constitution merely by using the Interfold. Participants opt in
through specified governance actions, including:

- Locking FOLD.
- Submitting or voting on proposals.
- Receiving payments made under DAO Resolutions.

## Governance parameters

This table is the reference for the live Interfold DAO configuration.

| Parameter                                 | Current setting                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Governance asset                          | FOLD                                                      |
| Voting-power model                        | 1 active FOLD = 1 vote                                    |
| Locked FOLD                               | Counts after delegation                                   |
| Bonded FOLD                               | Counts automatically                                      |
| Vesting FOLD                              | Counts automatically                                      |
| Lock withdrawal cooldown                  | 30 days                                                   |
| Voting power after withdrawal starts      | Stops immediately                                         |
| Standard proposal type                    | IPP                                                       |
| Standard voting                           | Receipt-free secret ballot through CRISP                  |
| Fallback proposal type                    | FIPP                                                      |
| Fallback voting                           | Transparent on-chain voting                               |
| Foundation approval                       | Board review and approval required before implementation  |
| Minimum voting power to vote              | No additional minimum beyond active voting power          |
| Minimum voting power to create a proposal | 12K FOLD                                                  |
| Ballot interval                           | 5 days from the fixed voting start                        |
| Voting opens                              | At the scheduled start, after the worst-case key deadline |
| New vote commitment cutoff                | At the scheduled voting end                               |
| Avail finalization window                 | 3 hours after ballot commitments close                    |
| Interfold input window                    | Voting start through Avail finalization                   |
| Guaranteed voting time                    | 5 days                                                    |
| Minimum participation / quorum            | 2%                                                        |
| Approval threshold                        | >50%                                                      |
| Execution delay                           | None                                                      |

The CRISP program checks part of this timing when the E3 for a round is requested:

- A round that opens voting before the worst-case key deadline is refused.
- A round that leaves less than one hour for new vote commitments is refused.
- New vote commitments close one Avail finalization window before the end of the E3 input window.
  The CRISP deploy script sets this window to 10,800 seconds (3 hours).
