# FOLD Token

This FAQ explains what FOLD does in the Interfold network. It is for token holders and for people
who want to operate a ciphernode. The questions are in three groups: the role of FOLD,
transferability, and ciphernode operation. For supply and allocation data, see
[FOLD Tokenomics](/tokenomics).

## The role of FOLD

### What role does FOLD play in the Interfold network?

The Interfold is a distributed network for confidential coordination. Applications request Encrypted
Execution Environments (E3s). Ciphernodes coordinate and secure each computation.

FOLD supports the economic operation of the network.

### What is the utility of the FOLD token?

FOLD has three main functions, and the three functions are interlocked: ciphernode bonding, protocol
incentives, and governance.

**Ciphernode bonding.** Ciphernode operators bond FOLD to become eligible for network duties. Bonded
FOLD stays in the `BondingRegistry` contract. When the bond owner (the wallet that funds the bond)
unbonds it, the FOLD waits in an exit queue before the bond owner can claim it. Bonded FOLD may be
subject to protocol-defined withdrawal periods. It may also be subject to penalties for provable
misbehavior or for failure to fulfill assigned responsibilities. These mechanisms align operational
responsibility with economic accountability.

**Protocol incentives.** Ciphernode operators receive a share of the fees of completed E3s. In
addition, they may receive FOLD incentives for successfully completing protocol-defined duties as
members of an E3 committee.

**Governance.** FOLD is expected to support governance participation through the Interfold DAO.
Governance may include decisions about network parameters, protocol upgrades, and treasury
administration. Governance processes may include secret-ballot mechanisms that the Interfold network
powers. For the current voting rules, see [Governance](/governance).

## Transferability

### When does FOLD become generally transferable?

General FOLD transferability can be enabled at or after Aug 19 at **14:00 UTC (10 AM ET)**.

At or after Unix timestamp **`1787148000`**, anyone can call the `tge()` function on the FOLD
contract to enable general transferability.

When that transaction is executed, FOLD becomes generally transferable.

General FOLD transferability and Network Alpha are distinct milestones in the broader Interfold
launch sequence. For more information, see [FOLD Transferability](/faq/transferability).

## Ciphernode operation

### How can I use FOLD to operate a ciphernode?

Operating a ciphernode is designed to be permissionless. The setup has four parts:

1. Provision cloud-based or personal hardware.
2. Run the ciphernode software.
3. Bond FOLD to the ciphernode.
4. Complete on-chain registration.

When the ciphernode is registered, it can participate in E3 committees when the protocol rules
select it. To be active for committee selection, a registered ciphernode also needs a minimum ticket
balance. See [Tickets and sortition](/operate/tickets-and-sortition).

For current setup requirements, bonding instructions, and operator documentation, see
[Ciphernode Operators](/operate) and [Registration and bonding](/operate/registration).

### Can I delegate my FOLD to a ciphernode operator?

No. The bonding model of the Interfold is deliberately operator-only. Every FOLD that is bonded to a
ciphernode is capital at risk for the operator that runs it.

Delegated capital would let operators run partially-owned ciphernodes. That would dilute
accountability and weaken the economic security guarantees of the network.

In the `BondingRegistry` contract, each ciphernode has one bond owner, and only the bond owner can
bond FOLD to it. The operator sets the bond owner. The bond owner can be the operator address or a
separate wallet, for example a cold wallet or a Safe.
